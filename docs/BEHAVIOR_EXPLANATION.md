# Explaining a pet's behaviour in context

Status: 2026-10-30 (E32; earlier sections 2026-10-20 - the machine clock
was reset between the two milestones, see `docs/STATUS.json`). Code:
`src/petperception/understand/`; command: `petperception explain`
(default: the E31 system, `--system-version 2`: the E32 system); evaluations:
E31 (`src/petperception/eval/e31_behavior_questions.py`, `results/e31/`) and
E32 (`src/petperception/eval/e32_behavior_v2.py`, `results/e32/`; §10-11).

## 1. The task

The user gives the species (cat or dog; trusted, never overridden), a short
scene description and a question, together with a recording, for example:

> In an open living room, my cat keeps turning in circles while biting its
> own tail. What could this behavior mean?

The system must:
1. locate the animal (detection and a single-target track; the detector's
   own species vote is reported, never used to reject the user's species);
2. recognize what the animal does and when (behaviour bouts, temporal
   pattern: staying in place, turning, repetition);
3. combine this with the scene and the question;
4. answer with **candidate explanations grounded in cited sources**, keeping
   requests and intentions apart from play, arousal or stress, compulsive and
   medical explanations, without forcing a human-like intention onto every
   behaviour, and without claiming that a cause is established;
5. say what would tell the candidates apart, which signs need a
   veterinarian, and how uncertain each part is.

No published system was found that takes a home recording of a cat or dog
plus an owner's question and returns source-grounded candidate explanations.
The two halves exist separately: general animal action recognition (mostly
wild animals) and the veterinary-behaviour literature (no automatic
recognition). This work joins them with a deliberately simple, inspectable
middle layer.

## 4. Method

```
media ─ decode 6 fps (≤ 60 s, ≤ 480 px) ─ locate: phone detector, cat or dog boxes, single-target track with gap filling
      ├─ motion in the animal's region (optical flow: speed, rotation, share moving) + appearance (repetition)
      ├─ SigLIP image embeddings of the animal crop at 2 fps, scene embedding
      └─ YAMNet scores of the sound (when present)
recognize ─ zero-shot per-frame probabilities over the visual behaviours (16; 14 per species) + neutral states (prompts in vocab.py)
          ├─ bouts (frames where a behaviour is the most likely reading, p ≥ 0.35)
          ├─ optional trained head (logistic regression on pooled embeddings; E31: not adopted)
          └─ temporal pattern (stays in place, turning, repetition) - descriptive
question ─ deterministic parsing (aliases, pronoun normalization, crude stemming) → reported behaviours, age, repetition
knowledge ─ entry per (behaviour, species): candidates by kind with sources, context cue words, distinguishing observations, urgent signs
answer ─ what was seen (with times) · what the user reported and whether the recording shows it · candidates by kind, ordered by
         the user's description · request/intention possible or not · observations that separate them · urgent signs · uncertainty · sources
```

Design rules (enforced by `knowledge.validate()` and the tests):
* every candidate outside "ordinary play or maintenance" cites a source with
  its tier (peer-reviewed study or case report, veterinary school, clinical
  review, veterinarian-reviewed advice, animal-welfare organization);
* *kind* separates `request` (the animal seeks something from a person,
  including "stop touching me") from `play_or_normal`, `arousal_or_stress`,
  `compulsive` and `medical`; a behaviour without a request candidate gets an
  explicit "no request is implied" note;
* context cue words change the order of candidates, never their content;
* the answer states that the recording cannot establish why;
* the user's species is used even when the detector votes otherwise (the
  disagreement is reported).

```bash
uv sync --extra dev --extra train --extra behavior     # torch (CPU), transformers
# models: SigLIP base patch16-224 in var/models/siglip_base_patch16_224 (see DATA_AND_MODEL_REGISTER.md)
petperception explain clip.mp4 --species cat \
  --scene "open living room" \
  --question "my cat keeps turning in circles while biting its own tail - what could this mean?" \
  --out answer.json --markdown answer.md
# E32 system (recording context, turning-in-place model, parser v2); its sentence encoder
# all-MiniLM-L6-v2 goes to var/models/minilm_l6_v2 (manifests/resources.json; without it, keyword parsing only)
```


## 7. Results (E31, measured; `results/e31/metrics.json`)

All numbers below are measured on real recordings. The protocol, targets and
benchmark were committed before any score (3925a50). One deviation from
"nothing seen before the run": a crash test of the evaluation code on random

### 7.1 Recognition on the pool (human labels, 5-fold grouped cross-validation)

278 clips with a located animal (of 300; 21 without, 1 duplicate removed),
131 groups (uploaders or papers). Nine behaviours have at least 8 positive
clips from at least 3 groups.

| Recognizer | Macro AP over 9 behaviours [95 % CI, cluster bootstrap] |
| --- | --- |
| R0 zero-shot SigLIP (frozen) | **0.416 [0.351, 0.496]** |
| R1 trained head (logistic regression on pooled embeddings) | 0.248 [0.210, 0.321] |
| R2 mean of R0 and R1 | 0.407 [0.356, 0.485] |
| chance (mean prevalence) | 0.073 |

Gain over R0: R1 -0.167 [-0.243, -0.088], R2 -0.009 [-0.056, +0.038].
Neither qualifies (gain >= 0.03 with the interval above 0): **the zero-shot
recognizer stays; no trained head is deployed.** With about 10-46 weakly
labelled clips per behaviour, from very different footage, a linear head on
1,537 inputs overfits; the frozen encoder's prompts generalize better.

### 7.4 Example (q02, abridged; all 37 answers in `results/e31/answers.md`)

Question: "My 4-year-old German Shepherd chases his tail many times a day,
usually while he waits for his food or when I talk with other people. Why
does he do this?" The answer reports tail chasing in the recording
(0.0-15.5 s and 17.5-23.0 s; also a bark at 12.5 s), lists five candidates
in order of fit to the description - frustration or arousal in waiting
situations (Tiira 2012), canine compulsive disorder (Tiira 2012, Burn 2011;
"German shepherd" matched), self-directed play, a learned attention-getting
behaviour (request; Burn 2011), a physical or neurological cause
(Van Haevermaet 2026, Frontiers 2026) - and asks when it started, whether it
can be interrupted, in which situations it starts, whether the tail is
injured, and whether there are other repetitive behaviours. It also shows
a false recognition: "threat display" at 3.5 and 14.5 s.

The G1b detector (run B, finished in this milestone; selected over G1a on
development data, acceptance 1 met, see TRAINING_PROGRAM.md) replaced the
phone detector in the `locate` step and every clip was re-processed. Same
protocol, same clips; descriptive (no adoption decision here).

| Locate with | Mean share of frames with the animal located (299 clips) | Pool clips with a located animal | Zero-shot macro AP [95 % CI] | Benchmark B1 (tail chasing) | Confusers rejected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phone detector (default) | 0.727 | 278 | 0.416 [0.351, 0.496] | 22/36 (3/8) | 5/6 |
| G1b at the locate threshold 0.25 (pre-registered) | **0.786** (83 clips located in more frames, 20 in fewer) | 275 | 0.404 [0.347, 0.481] | 23/36 (3/8) | 5/6 |

## 9. Gaps and next steps (after E32)

* **Parsing is the main failure on fresh questions** (C1 16/33 for v2; the
  E31 questions, written alongside the aliases, flattered the parser:
  27/34 there). Missed paraphrases are listed in `results/e32/posthoc.json`.
  A v3 parser needs a larger development set of independently written
  paraphrases (ideally owner-written) and must be tested on new questions.
* **New behaviours need their own thresholds**: head tremor, collapse and
  rolling use the fixed zero-shot default 0.30 and fire on unrelated clips
  (head tremor 7 false alarms in 37 clips). Development positives exist only
  as single paper cases, so these behaviours should be reported from the
  recording only after a development set supports a threshold.
* **Tail chasing**: v2 met the pre-registered criterion on 2 + 2 clips, but
  the clip decision used the threshold chosen for zero-shot scores (0.64)
  on the combined score; at the threshold development chose for the combined
  score (0.81) neither positive passes. Not established as reliable; needs
  more fresh tail-chasing recordings and a single consistent threshold.
* **Recording context**: the "person close to the animal" rule accepts
  person boxes at detector score 0.3, and 7 of the 12 false reports never
  reached 0.5; brief detections and the animal itself misread as a person
  explain the rest. A v3 should use the 0.5 score and a minimum duration,
  chosen on development clips. Place prompts over-call "veterinary clinic"
  (6 of 10 such calls were wrong).
* **Cue words match inside other words** ("ear" in "2-year-old" ranked
  ear discomfort first in t06; "bit" in other words was noted in E31):
  matching should respect word boundaries except for stems marked as such.
* **Gap answers** should say explicitly that the behaviour is outside the
  knowledge base instead of only listing what was seen.
* **Data**: still few tail-chasing recordings with usable terms; questions
  are written by the assistant from captions, not collected from owners;
  frame-review references (person, place, bouts) are machine-assisted.
* **Phone**: unchanged - SigLIP (812 MB) and MiniLM exceed the phone budget;
  nothing here has been run on a phone.

## 10. Version 2 (E32, 2026-10-30): what changed and why

Each change except the last answers a measured E31 failure; everything was
developed on the E31 collection only, then frozen before the fresh E32 test
material was labelled (the last change was made knowing the test topics).

| E31 finding | v2 change | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tail chasing recognized in 3/8 clips; the detector loses spinning animals; single frames do not show circling | feature version 2 bridges detector gaps up to 2 s and holds the last box 1 s; a window model of the track over time (staying in place, box-shape oscillation, horizontal-motion flips, missed detections, zero-shot circling probability) replaces the frame score for tail chasing | `understand/features.py`, `understand/temporal.py` |
| Threat displays 0/2 from video | hiss or growl in the sound (YAMNet >= 0.3) is evidence for a threat display next to the visual reading; the answer names the channel | `recognize.recognize_v2` |
| Context only from the user's words | context observed in the recording - a person close to the animal, bowls and toys, furniture, bathroom fittings, place (scene prompts), laughter, people talking, doorbell - listed with instrument and score, and allowed to reorder candidates with half the weight of the user's words (laughter matches the "owners laugh, which can reinforce it" candidate of Burn 2011) | `understand/context.py` |
| No temporal relation between events | "before and after": other recognized behaviours and recording context within 5 s of each bout | `context.events_around` |
| Parsing 27/34; "after eating" read as a reported behaviour | after / before / until phrases become context; each clause may add its best paraphrase match from a frozen sentence encoder (threshold and margin chosen on a separate development question set) | `understand/question.py`, `benchmarks/parser_dev.json` |
| Not covered by knowledge base v1: neurological circling, head bobbing, sleep twitching, collapse, rolling and rubbing (not E31 findings: chosen because the selected E32 test papers show these conditions, see §11) | knowledge base v2 entries with sources independent of the E32 test papers (Merck, dvm360, Veterinary Partner, MSPCA-Angell, AKC, VCA, Vet Times, Bol 2017) | `understand/knowledge.py` (33 sources) |

Development results (E31 collection only; `results/e32/development.json`):
the turning-in-place window model alone reached an out-of-fold clip AP of
0.30 for tail chasing (8 positive clips among 294), the zero-shot score
0.35, their mean 0.43 (adopted; 3 of 8 positives and 1 false positive at the
development threshold 0.81). Re-running the E31 part A rule on feature
version 2 kept the zero-shot recognizer (trained head -0.19 macro AP).
Parser v2 passed 66 of 84 development questions (keyword parser: 53).

**Decision (pre-registered rule): v2 does not become the default.** Over
all 37 questions v2 is better than v1 on every paired measure (an asked behaviour was recognized
only by v2 in 5 questions and only by v1 in 2; parsing passed only for v2 in
5 questions and only for v1 in 2; expert coverage +3; ranking +4),
but the rule also required C2-C4 and C6-C9 to reach 0.9 for v2, and C4 is
28/33 = 0.85. The default of `petperception explain` therefore stays the E31
system. Its code is kept verbatim in `understand/e31/` (a test pins the file
hashes), and `results/e32/default_v1_check.json` shows that the default
reproduces the 37 tested v1 answers (33 differ only by the recording-quality
notes the E31 `Explainer` adds and the evaluation runner did not). v2 stays
available with `--system-version 2`. The C4 failures are all questions in
which the asked behaviour was neither parsed nor recognized, so nothing was
explained (t08 sleep movements, o13 drinking, o20, o21 running, o23 ball in
the river).

**Where v2's advantage comes from (post hoc, `posthoc.json`
"without_topic_matched_questions").** Knowledge base v2 added behaviours and
candidates (head tremor, sleep movements, collapse, rolling / rubbing, the
neurological-circling candidate) for conditions that the chosen test papers
cover: the test material and the new entries were registered together
(300eab5) before development, so the topics - not the clips or the
questions - were known. The sources are independent of the test papers,
but the five questions on those conditions (t03, t06, t08, t09, t12) favour
v2 by construction; the circling candidate's cue words ("to the left",
"can't be distracted") also match the hamartoma question's wording. **On
the other 32 questions the two systems tie on every explanation check**
(parse 13/28 each, request separation 24/28 each, expert coverage 2/2
each) **and v2 recognizes 20 vs 21 of 28 asked behaviours** (tail chasing
+1, staring and resting -1 each). E32 therefore shows a coverage gain for
the conditions added on purpose, not that parser v2, recording context or
the window model improve answers in general.

**Tail chasing (pre-registered criterion met, not established as reliable).**
v2 recognized both fresh tail-chasing clips (the Internet Archive cat, score
0.80; the sensory-impaired dog, 0.637) and rejected both tail-chasing
confusers (the hamartoma dog turning its head, a corgi chasing a ball). With
n = 2 and 2 the intervals run from 0.34 to 1.00. Found after scoring
(`results/e32/posthoc.json`): the dog passed the threshold by 0.001, and the
threshold applied (0.64) was the one the part A rule chose for zero-shot
scores, while development chose 0.81 for the combined score - at 0.81
neither positive would pass. v2 also called tail chasing in a corgi running
through snow (0.667). The v1 default recognized 1 of 2.

**Post-hoc, descriptive** (`results/e32/posthoc.json`; nothing changed
because of it):
* behaviours reported but absent from the reference and the frame review:
  29 of 56 for v2 (head tremor 7, collapse 2) and 24 of 49 for v1;
* parse: v2 missed the asked behaviour entirely in 13 questions (v1 18)
  and added an unasked one in 6 (v1 1), e.g. "shakes and bites his teddy
  bear" -> head tremor, "wiping her face" -> rolling / rubbing;
* person: no person missed; 12 false reports, 7 of them from person boxes
  that never reached detector score 0.5 (the "close to the animal" rule
  accepts 0.3);
* place: right in 13 of 24; "veterinary clinic" said 10 times, wrong 6.

Example (t06, v2; all answers in `results/e32/answers.md`, v1 answers in
`results/e32/v1_answers.json`): "My 2-year-old Schnauzer's head starts
bobbing round and round while he is wide awake, and it seems to stop when I
talk to him. Should I worry?" - v2 recognizes head tremor in the recording
(4.0-9.0 s and three shorter bouts) and offers three medical candidates: ear
discomfort, idiopathic head tremor, and a focal seizure as the main
alternative (MSPCA-Angell). It asks whether the movement stops when the dog
is called or offered a treat and lists unresponsiveness, drooling, collapse
or loss of bladder control as reasons to see a veterinarian promptly. It also
shows a known defect: ear discomfort is ranked first because the cue word
"ear" matches inside "2-year-old" (substring matching, §9). v1 had no entry
for the behaviour and reported grooming.
